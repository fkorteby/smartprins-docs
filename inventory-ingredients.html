Step 01 — Define the Menu Building the Foundation: Your Menu Items and Raw Materials

The first step in setting up your inventory system is building your menu. Every item your customers can order through the POS must exist as a product in the system before recipes, ingredients, or stock tracking can be configured.

This step has no prerequisites. You start here.

Overview

Products are the final menu items your customers order. Creating them here is what allows the rest of the system to work — recipes get attached to products, and every POS sale references a product to know which ingredients to deduct from stock.

How to Access

Navigate to Menu → Products from the left sidebar.

This opens the Products list view, where all existing products are displayed with their name, Arabic name, category, and status. From here you can search, filter by category or status, and manage existing items.

The Products list view showing all menu items with their category and status tags. Use the grid or list toggle on the top right to switch views.

Adding a New Product

Click the + Add Product button in the top left of the Products list to open the Add Product form.

The Add Product form opens as a modal with five tabs: Informations, Options, Suggestions, Recipe, and admin_nutritions. All required setup for inventory purposes happens in the Informations and Recipe tabs.

The form is organized into five tabs. For the purposes of menu setup in this first-time configuration, focus on the Informations tab first.

Once all required fields are filled in, click Submit to save the product. It will immediately appear in the Products list and become available for recipe assignment. To discard the entry without saving, click Close.

Tips for Efficient Menu Setup

  • If you have a large product catalog, you can upload your menu in bulk using an Excel file. Prepare your menu items in the provided Excel template, then upload the file to automatically create all products at once. This significantly speeds up the initial setup and reduces manual entry errors.
  • Make sure every product has its Status set to Enabled and a category assigned. Products without a category may not appear correctly on the POS or in reports.
  • You do not need to fill in the Recipe tab at this stage. Recipe assignment is covered in Step 04. Focus here on getting every active product created with the correct name, category, price, and status.

Step 02 — Define Your Ingredients

Adding ingredients is the most important step in setting up your inventory. If the ingredients are set up correctly, your recipes, purchase orders, and cost reports will work perfectly. If not, mistakes here will ripple through your entire system.

What Counts as an Ingredient?

Yes: Anything you buy from a supplier (flour, sugar, packaging) OR anything you make in-house from raw materials to use later (pizza dough, house sauce).

No: Final items sold directly to customers at the register. Those are "Products" (Step 01).

How to Access

Navigate to Menu → Stock → Ingredients from the left sidebar, then click the + Add Ingredient button.

The Ingredients list view. All created ingredients appear here with their key attributes visible at a glance. Use the search bar and filters to locate specific items once your list grows.

The Setup Form — Field by Field

When you add an ingredient, you will fill out a form. Here is what each part means:

FieldWhat you need to doWhy it matters
NameGive it a clear, consistent name (e.g., Milk - Full Fat instead of just Milk).Prevents duplicate entries and keeps your lists organized as you grow.
Unit of MeasurementChoose how you use the item in recipes, not how you buy it (e.g., use grams, not kilogram bags).Crucial: Changing this later will break your existing stock data and recipes.
CostEnter the cost for one single base unit (e.g., the cost of 1 gram, not the 1kg bag).This calculates your exact food costs and profit margins when a dish is sold.
StatusSet to Enabled for items you are currently using.Setting it to Disabled hides it from the system without deleting your historical data.
CompositeTurn this ON if the ingredient is made up of other ingredients (like a spice blend).Allows the system to calculate the cost based on the raw materials used to make it.
TrackableTurn this ON so the system counts it.If this is off, the system won't deduct stock when you sell food, ruining your inventory numbers.
PreparableTurn this ON if your kitchen makes this item in large batches to use later (like bread dough). Items marked as preparable should be set as composite to track their stock.Deducts the components consumed and adds the prepared item to stock, keeping its levels accurately tracked.

Pro Tips for a Smooth Setup

  • Group your work: Enter all your dairy, then all your meats, then all your dry goods. It makes spotting mistakes much easier.
  • Never delete, just disable: If you stop using an ingredient, change its status to Disabled. Deleting an item that has been used in past recipes or orders can cause system errors.
  • Double-check your math: Entering the cost of a whole bulk box instead of the cost-per-gram is the most common mistake. Always convert the price to your base unit.
Important: The Unit of Measurement field cannot be reliably changed after stock activity has been recorded. Set it correctly the first time.
