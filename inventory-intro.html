Core Building Blocks

Understanding how the system categorizes your items is the most important step before setting anything up. These three terms are not interchangeable.

Ingredients, Preparations & Products

Category What is it? Examples How the System Treats It
Ingredients Raw materials you buy directly from your suppliers. Flour, milk, raw chicken, sugar. These are tracked in your stock. You buy them, and they are used up when you cook.
Preparations Sub-recipes or batches made in-house to be used in final dishes. Pizza dough, house BBQ sauce, marinated chicken. You can't buy these. You make them using Ingredients, and then they are used to build Products.
Products The final menu items that customers order and pay for. A pepperoni pizza, a chicken sandwich, a latte. These do not hold stock. They are linked to a recipe that tells the system which Ingredients to deduct when sold.

Stock vs. Consumption

These two terms describe the "having" and the "using" of your inventory.

Term What it means The Best Analogy
Stock The live quantity of an item you currently have on hand. It goes up when deliveries arrive and down when food is sold or wasted. It is your Bank Balance — your current snapshot of what is available right now.
Consumption The record of how much of an ingredient was used. This is usually triggered automatically when a product is sold at the register. It is your Transaction History — the list of what was spent to get your balance to where it is today.
Key insight: The system handles consumption automatically through the POS. Your team's job is to manage everything else — purchases received, waste logged, transfers processed, and counts submitted — so the stock balance stays accurate.
